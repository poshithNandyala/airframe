Comparison

A better picture-in-picture alternative for Windows

Picture-in-picture is a small window with a short leash. If you have outgrown it, these are the Windows options worth comparing, and the trade-offs that matter.

By Poshith Nandyala Updated November 25, 2026 4 min read
Short answer

Browser picture-in-picture is great for a quick clip, but it is small, limited to one video and has no opacity, click-through or screen-share shield. Airframe is a full YouTube window that can shrink to PiP size while keeping those controls.

Picture-in-picture solved a real problem: you can keep a video visible while you work in another window. But browser PiP was designed as a lightweight escape hatch, not as a permanent desktop player. Once you use it every day, the limits show up quickly.

This guide compares the realistic alternatives on Windows 10 and 11, including when browser PiP is still the right choice.

What browser picture-in-picture gets right

  • Zero install. Chrome, Edge, Firefox and most Chromium browsers support it.
  • Always on top. The PiP window stays above normal windows without any configuration.
  • Fast. Two clicks or a keyboard shortcut, and the video is floating.
  • Cross-platform. The same habit works on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS.

For a five-minute clip during a call, that is hard to beat.

Where picture-in-picture falls short

  • It is small and fiddly. You can resize it, but the controls are minimal and the window is easy to lose.
  • It is video-only. No search, playlists, recommendations, comments or channel pages. To change the video you go back to the tab.
  • One video per browser. A second PiP usually replaces the first.
  • No opacity. The video is either visible at full strength or closed.
  • No click-through. The floating box still catches clicks, so it can block the app underneath.
  • No screen-share shield. Depending on the capture method, the PiP window can appear in a share.
  • It dies with the tab. Close the browser and the video goes with it.

None of that makes PiP bad. It makes it a snack, not a meal.

Why Airframe is a different kind of PiP replacement

Airframe does not try to float a single video element. It opens the real YouTube site in a borderless window and then gives that window desktop powers:

  • Full YouTube when you want it. Search, playlists, subscriptions and settings are all there. Resize the window down to 400×225 when you want the PiP feel.
  • Any opacity. Fade the video to a hint over your editor. The window remains interactive until you enable click-through.
  • True click-through. Bring your editor forward, press AltL, and your pointer works through the video. This is the part PiP cannot do.
  • Screen-share shield. The window is excluded from most Windows capture paths and is on by default.
  • Global shortcuts. Hide, fullscreen, opacity, monitor switching and click-through work while another app has focus.
  • No browser attached. No tabs, address bar or taskbar button. The YouTube session lives in its own local profile.

It is still a web view, so YouTube's own changes apply. That is a feature: you get the real site, not a cut-down clone.

When browser PiP is still the better choice

  • You need a video for two minutes and do not want to install anything.
  • You are on macOS, Linux or ChromeOS, where Airframe does not run.
  • You want to float a non-YouTube site that supports PiP.
  • You are on a locked-down work machine that blocks new applications.
  • You need the lowest possible resource use and the browser is already open.

How to switch from PiP to Airframe

  1. Download and run the Airframe installer, or unzip the portable build.
  2. Open YouTube from the launcher and sign in if you want your subscriptions.
  3. Resize the window to the size you used for PiP. A 16:9 box around 400×225 is a good match.
  4. Set opacity with AltShift↑ or ↓.
  5. Bring your work app to the front and press AltL for click-through.

You can keep browser PiP installed. Nothing conflicts, and it remains useful for the rest of the web.

The bottom line

If you want the smallest possible floating video with no install, browser PiP is still excellent. If you want a YouTube window that can be small or large, fade, click through and stay out of screen shares, Airframe is the more capable alternative on Windows.

Frequently asked questions

Is Airframe a replacement for picture-in-picture?

For YouTube on Windows, yes. Airframe is a full YouTube window that can be resized to a small floating box or run large, with opacity, click-through and a screen-share shield. Browser PiP still works and remains useful for non-YouTube sites.

Does Airframe support Netflix, Prime Video or Twitch?

Not yet. YouTube is ready today. Prime Video, Netflix and Twitch appear in the launcher as coming soon.

Is Airframe free?

Yes. Airframe is free and open source under the MIT license. The installer and portable ZIP are on GitHub Releases.

Can I use Airframe and browser PiP at the same time?

Yes. They are independent. You could keep a YouTube video in Airframe and a different site in browser PiP, subject to your GPU and screen space.

Does Airframe play rented YouTube movies?

Rented or DRM-only movies may not play because Electron does not include Google Widevine. Normal YouTube videos and Premium playback work.
