YouTube mini player on Windows 11

The mini player inside YouTube is not the same as a floating Windows window. Here is the difference, why the built-in one disappears, and how to get the behavior you actually wanted.

By Poshith Nandyala Updated November 25, 2026 4 min read
Short answer

YouTube's mini player lives inside the YouTube website or app. On Windows 11 it is not a system-wide always-on-top window. For a real floating mini player, use Airframe, resize it to a 16:9 box, and pin it above your work.

Search for "YouTube mini player Windows 11" and you will find two different things: YouTube's own mini player inside the website, and the small floating window people actually want on their desktop. They are not the same feature, and the gap explains most of the confusion.

What YouTube's mini player actually is

On youtube.com, start a video and then browse to another YouTube page. YouTube shrinks the playing video into a small player in the corner so the audio and video continue while you look at something else on the site. On mobile, the YouTube app has a similar mini player that can keep playing while you use other apps, depending on your subscription and settings.

On Windows, the web mini player is still part of the browser tab. It is not a Windows-level window. It has no taskbar button, no always-on-top flag and no presence outside the browser. If you switch to your editor, the mini player goes behind it with the rest of the browser.

Why the built-in mini player feels broken on Windows

  • It is trapped in the tab. The browser must stay open, and the tab must stay alive.
  • It is not always on top. It sits in normal window order, so your work covers it.
  • It cannot be moved outside the browser. You can move it around the page, not around the desktop.
  • It is not a system media window. It does not appear in the taskbar or respond to global shortcuts.
  • Tab discarding can end it. Browsers unload background tabs to save memory, especially on laptops.

YouTube Premium does not change this. Premium removes ads and enables background playback in the mobile app; it does not add a Windows desktop mini player.

Make Airframe behave like a mini player

  1. Install and open YouTube. Use the installer or portable ZIP from the download section.
  2. Pick a video or playlist. A playlist keeps the mini player useful after the first video ends.
  3. Resize to 16:9. Drag an edge until the window is roughly 400×225 or 480×270. The top strip and edges are draggable.
  4. Park it in a corner. Bottom-right is the classic spot; top-right works well if your editor has a minimap or terminal on the right.
  5. Set opacity. AltShift↓ fades it. 70–90% keeps the video readable; lower values make it more peripheral.
  6. Turn on click-through. Bring your work app to the front, then press AltL. Your pointer now works through the mini player.

Sizing and placement tips

Sixteen-by-nine sizes that scale cleanly:

  • 320×180: a true mini player; best for music and ambient video.
  • 400×225: readable for talking-head videos and tutorials.
  • 480×270: a good compromise for a laptop screen.
  • 640×360: large enough to follow a demo without taking half the display.

Keep the window away from the corner where your OS shows notifications, and away from any app controls you use constantly. If it covers something, click-through is faster than moving it.

What happens at 0% opacity

At 0% the window is invisible and click-through, so it cannot block your work. Audio keeps playing. AltShift↑ brings it back. This is the closest Airframe gets to an audio-only mode, and it is useful for music, podcasts and long livestreams you only want to hear.

The bottom line

YouTube's mini player is a convenience inside the site, not a Windows feature. If you want a small video that stays above your work, responds to global shortcuts, and can fade or click through, use a dedicated floating window. Airframe is free, and a 400×225 window is a near-perfect PiP replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the mini player on YouTube desktop?

On youtube.com, start a video and browse to another YouTube page. The small player appears in the corner of the site. It is part of the page, not a Windows overlay, so it stays inside the browser window.

Why does the YouTube mini player disappear on Windows 11?

Because it belongs to the browser tab. Closing the tab, navigating away from YouTube, or letting the browser discard the tab ends it. It also sits behind other windows unless the browser itself is on top.

Can I resize the YouTube mini player?

The in-page mini player has a fixed small size. Browser picture-in-picture can be resized within limits. An Airframe window can be resized freely, including down to a 320x180 or 400x225 box.

Does Airframe keep the mini player on top of other apps?

Yes. Airframe is always on top by default, so the video stays above your editor, browser or game in borderless windowed mode.

Is Airframe free on Windows 11?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license. Windows 11 and Windows 10 are both supported.
