How to hide a window from screen share on Windows

Windows can exclude a window from capture without hiding it from you. Here is how the shield works, which apps respect it, and where the limits are.

By Poshith Nandyala Updated November 25, 2026 4 min read
Short answer

On Windows 10 (2004 or newer) and Windows 11, an app can ask Windows to exclude its window from screen capture. Airframe does this by default. Press Alt+S to toggle the shield. It works with most standard capture tools, but not with a camera pointed at your screen or external capture hardware.

Sharing your screen should not mean sharing everything on it. A YouTube video, a personal message, a half-finished document or a tab you forgot about can end up in a client call or a recorded demo. Windows has a mechanism for this, and Airframe exposes it as a one-key shield.

How Windows screen sharing sees your screen

Most Windows capture tools do not read the physical display directly. They ask the Desktop Window Manager (DWM) for a composed image, or they capture a specific window through the Windows Graphics Capture API. Windows lets an app mark its own window with a display affinity value that tells DWM how the window should appear to those capture paths.

The relevant API is SetWindowDisplayAffinity. The strongest option, WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE, makes the window invisible to capture while it remains visible on the physical display. Electron wraps this as win.setContentProtection(true), which is what Airframe calls.

In practice, a captured desktop often shows a black rectangle where the protected window sits. Some tools crop it out or show the desktop behind it. The exact result depends on the capture API and the app doing the capturing.

What Airframe's screen-share shield does

  • On by default. You do not have to enable it before a call.
  • Toggles with AltS. A toast confirms whether the shield is on or off.
  • Protects the Airframe window only. It does not hide your other apps, notifications or desktop.
  • Keeps the video visible to you. The window is excluded from capture, not from your own display.
  • Works alongside click-through and opacity. You can fade the video and still keep it out of a share.

Hide YouTube during a Teams, Zoom or Meet call

  1. Open the video in Airframe. Start playback before you join the call so you can confirm the window looks right.
  2. Check the shield. Press AltS if you are unsure. The toast should say the shield is on.
  3. Join and share. Choose the screen or window you normally share.
  4. Verify. Ask a colleague, start a solo meeting, or make a local recording. Confirm the video is absent or black in the capture.
  5. If it still appears, share a single window. Sharing one app window is more predictable than sharing the entire desktop. The video is less likely to be included at all.

If the meeting is recorded, the recording follows the same capture path as the live share. Test the recording if the distinction matters.

Does it work with OBS?

OBS Display Capture normally respects Windows display affinity, so the Airframe window is excluded. OBS Window Capture of the Airframe window may show black. OBS Game Capture only captures the game, so the video is not included in the first place.

If you want viewers to see the video, turn the shield off with AltS and capture the window or display normally. If you want it hidden, leave the shield on and use Display Capture for the rest of the desktop.

What the shield cannot hide

Display affinity is a Windows compositor feature. Anything that bypasses the compositor is outside its scope:

  • A camera pointed at your screen. No software can hide from that.
  • External capture hardware. An HDMI capture card or a second PC sees the physical output.
  • Some remote-support and forensic tools. Kernel-level capture or a tool running with higher privileges may not honor the flag.
  • The UAC secure desktop. Windows draws it separately, above normal windows.
  • Older Windows 10 builds. The strongest exclusion needs Windows 10 version 2004 or newer.
  • Someone looking over your shoulder. Privacy from capture is not privacy from the room.

It also does not hide the fact that you are running an app. A viewer may see a black rectangle and ask about it. If you need a completely clean desktop, close or move the sensitive content before sharing.

Other ways to keep a window out of a share

  • Share one app window. The most reliable option, and it avoids notifications and other tabs.
  • Move the window to another monitor. This works only if you share a single display and the tool respects that choice.
  • Use a second desktop. Virtual desktops help with organization, but sharing the desktop you are on can still include it.
  • Close what you do not need. The oldest privacy control is still the best one.

Use it for privacy, not for deception

The shield is useful for personal content during work calls: a video in the background, a private message, a document that is not ready to share. It is not a way to misrepresent what you are doing, and it does not override your employer's screen-sharing or recording policies. Use it the way you would use a privacy screen or a closed door.

The bottom line

Windows can exclude a window from screen capture, and Airframe turns that into a default-on shield with a single shortcut. It covers the common tools — Teams, Zoom, Meet, OBS and most screenshots — but it is not undetectable and it cannot beat a camera. Test your exact setup once, then stop worrying about the video in the corner.

Frequently asked questions

Does Airframe hide YouTube from screen share?

Yes, in most standard capture paths. The shield uses Windows display affinity and is on by default. Teams, Zoom, Meet, OBS and most screenshot tools respect it. Test with your specific tool before relying on it in a meeting.

Does the shield hide the window from screenshots?

Most screenshots taken through the normal Windows desktop compositor exclude the window or show it as black. Kernel-level capture tools, external capture hardware and a phone camera are not covered.

Is the screen-share shield on by default?

Yes. Airframe starts with the shield enabled. Alt+S toggles it, and a toast confirms the new state.

Can viewers tell that a window is hidden?

They may see a black rectangle, an empty space, or nothing at all, depending on the capture tool. The goal is privacy, not invisibility magic. If a clean share matters, share a single app window instead of the entire desktop.

Does it work on Windows 10?

Yes. The strongest exclusion uses WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE, which requires Windows 10 version 2004 or newer. Windows 11 supports it. Older Windows 10 builds may fall back to a different protection mode.

Does it hide my whole desktop?

No. It only affects the Airframe window. Other apps, notifications and browser tabs remain visible in a full-screen share.
